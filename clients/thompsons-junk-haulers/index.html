Meet Marcus Thompson

The Man Behind the Trucks

I grew up in Mesa — went to Westwood High, played baseball at Mesa Community College, and spent summers helping my dad clear out rental properties he managed on the side. Somewhere between loading my third couch into a borrowed pickup and hearing a homeowner say "you just saved my life", I realized this was more than a side hustle.

I started Thompson's Junk Haulers in 2022 with one truck, a dolly, and a promise: show up on time, quote a fair price, and leave the place cleaner than we found it. Four years and 500+ jobs later, we've grown to two trucks and a three-man crew — but the promise hasn't changed.

What sets us apart? We actually care what happens to your stuff. Grandma's old dresser doesn't need to go to the landfill if someone at St. Vincent de Paul can use it. We donate what we can, recycle the rest, and only trash what's truly gone. It takes a little longer, but it's the right thing to do.

Frequently Asked Questions

We haul just about everything non-hazardous — furniture, appliances (we'll even disconnect your old washer/dryer), hot tubs, mattresses, yard waste, construction debris, and full property contents. The only things we can't take are hazardous materials (paint, chemicals, asbestos), medical waste, and tires (those require a certified recycler). Not sure if we can take your stuff? Text us a photo at (480) 555-1234 and we'll confirm in 2 minutes.
We price by volume — how much space your items take up in our truck. No hourly rates, no fuel surcharges, no "oh we forgot to mention" fees. Here's a rough guide: a single item like a couch or fridge runs $100-$175. A garage cleanout or half-truck load is $350-$500. A full truck load is $500-$800. Full property cleanouts (estates, foreclosures, hoarder houses) are quoted on-site because every one's different — usually $1,500-$4,000 depending on the size of the home. We always quote before we touch anything, and the quote is the price.
Most of the time, yes. If you call before noon, there's about a 90% chance we can get to you that same afternoon. We run Monday through Saturday, 6 AM to 7 PM, across the entire East Valley — Mesa, Tempe, Chandler, Gilbert, Scottsdale, Apache Junction, and Queen Creek. If same-day isn't available, we'll get you first thing the next morning. We also offer scheduled appointments if you need a specific time window — we'll give you a 15-minute arrival window, not a 4-hour one.
Hot tub removal is one of our most-requested services — we do 3-4 a month across the East Valley. Here's the process: we disconnect the electrical (we're not electricians, but we handle standard plug-in disconnects — if it's hardwired, you'll need an electrician first), break the tub down into manageable sections on-site, and haul everything away including the cover, steps, and base pad if needed. Most hot tub jobs take 2-3 hours and run $400-$700 depending on size and access. We're careful with your patio and landscaping — we lay down plywood to protect surfaces.
Estate and foreclosure cleanouts are our bread and butter — we work with realtors, property managers, and families across the East Valley. For estates, we take extra care: we'll set aside anything that looks like it might have sentimental or monetary value (jewelry, documents, photos, antiques) and let you review before we haul. For foreclosures, we work fast — we've cleared 3-bedroom homes in a single day. We provide before-and-after photos with every cleanout, and we carry $1M in liability insurance so the property is protected. We also offer "broom-clean" service where we sweep and mop after clearing — most cleanout companies don't do that.
This is something we take seriously. We partner with St. Vincent de Paul on Main Street, Goodwill's Mesa distribution center, and Sunshine Acres Children's Home for donations. Usable furniture, clothing, and household items go to them first. Metals go to a certified scrap yard in Mesa. Electronics go to an e-waste recycler in Tempe. Appliances with working compressors get their refrigerant properly recovered. On average, we divert about 60% of what we haul from the landfill. It takes more work than just dumping everything, but Marcus is big on doing it right.
