When should a tree be removed?

Not every tree needs to come down, and a good arborist will tell you that. But there are clear signs that removal is the safest option. Portland homeowners should watch for these indicators:

  • Dead or dying trees with significant crown dieback
  • Trunk cracks, cavities, or signs of internal decay
  • Leaning trees with exposed or heaving roots
  • Trees growing into power lines or too close to structures
  • Root damage from construction or soil grade changes
  • Storm-damaged trees with broken leaders or split crotches

If you are unsure whether your tree needs to be removed or can be saved through pruning or cabling, we offer free consultations. Our arborist will give you an honest assessment — we would rather preserve a tree when it is safe to do so.

Portland tree removal permits & regulations

The City of Portland has specific regulations governing tree removal. On developed residential properties, trees 12 inches or larger in diameter at breast height generally require a permit from the Bureau of Development Services. Trees in environmental overlay zones, conservation areas, or designated heritage trees have additional protections.

Street trees — those in the planting strip between the sidewalk and street — are managed by Portland Parks & Recreation Urban Forestry. Removing a street tree requires a separate permit and often a replacement planting plan.

We handle the entire permitting process for our Portland clients. We know which trees require permits, how to file the applications, and what mitigation or replanting may be required. This is one of the reasons Portland homeowners choose a professional tree service over a handyman with a chainsaw — the regulatory side alone can be a headache if you do not know the process.

// FAQ

Tree removal questions.

Common questions about tree removal in Portland, OR. Still have questions? Get in touch.

Tree removal cost in Portland typically ranges from $500 to $3,000+ and depends on several factors: tree height and trunk diameter, proximity to structures or power lines, accessibility for equipment, overall canopy spread, whether the tree is dead or alive, and the slope or terrain of your property. We provide free on-site estimates with transparent pricing.

Portland requires permits for removing trees over 12 inches in diameter on developed residential lots. Trees in environmental overlay zones or street trees have additional requirements through Portland Urban Forestry. We handle the entire permit process for you and know exactly what the city requires.

Tree removal can be performed year-round in Portland. Late fall through early spring (when trees are dormant) is often ideal because reduced foliage makes the job faster and less expensive. However, hazardous or dead trees should be removed immediately regardless of season. Portland's wet winters can make scheduling tighter, so booking early is recommended.

We offer several options: we can cut the wood into firewood-length rounds and leave it for you, haul everything away for disposal, or chip smaller branches on-site. Many Portland homeowners keep hardwood like oak and maple for firewood. All green waste we haul is recycled or composted — nothing goes to a landfill.
