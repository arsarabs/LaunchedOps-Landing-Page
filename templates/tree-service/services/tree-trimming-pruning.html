Expert pruning that protects tree health

Portland's urban forest is one of the city's greatest assets, and proper pruning is the single most important thing you can do to protect your trees. At Summit Tree Experts, every pruning job is led by an ISA Certified Arborist who understands tree biology — not just where to cut, but why each cut matters.

Poor pruning kills more trees in Portland than most diseases. Topping — the practice of cutting main branches back to stubs — is one of the most destructive things you can do to a tree. It triggers weak, rapid regrowth that is more susceptible to failure, creates massive wounds that invite decay, and permanently disfigures the tree's natural form. We never top trees. Instead, we use proper reduction cuts, thinning cuts, and removal cuts that maintain the tree's structural integrity and promote long-term health.

Our tree trimming and pruning services in Portland range from $200 for small ornamental trees to $1,500 for large canopy trees requiring extensive crown work. Every estimate is free and includes a detailed explanation of what we recommend and why.

Types of pruning we perform

Different trees at different stages of life need different types of pruning. Our arborists assess each tree individually and recommend the approach that will deliver the best results for your situation.

  • Crown cleaning — removing dead, dying, diseased, and broken branches
  • Crown thinning — selective removal to reduce density and improve airflow and light penetration
  • Crown reduction — reducing the overall size of the canopy using proper reduction cuts
  • Crown raising — removing lower branches for clearance over walkways, driveways, and structures
  • Structural pruning — developing strong branch architecture in young trees to prevent future problems
  • Vista pruning — selective trimming to improve or maintain views while preserving tree health

Portland's common trees and their pruning needs

Portland's diverse tree canopy includes species with very different pruning requirements. Douglas firs, the iconic conifers of the Pacific Northwest, rarely need pruning beyond deadwood removal and clearance from structures. They respond poorly to heavy pruning and should never be topped — yet we see this mistake across Portland neighborhoods constantly.

Bigleaf maples, one of Portland's most common deciduous trees, grow aggressively and often need crown reduction and thinning every 3-5 years to manage their size and prevent storm damage. Their dense canopy catches wind like a sail, and without proper thinning, large limbs can fail during Portland's fall and winter storms.

Ornamental trees like Japanese maples, cherries, and dogwoods require more delicate pruning focused on preserving their natural form. These trees are often over-pruned by well-meaning homeowners, leading to a stressed, unnatural appearance. Our arborists take a conservative approach that enhances the tree's beauty without over-cutting.

Why proper timing matters in Portland

The timing of pruning directly affects how well a tree recovers. In Portland's maritime climate, late winter through early spring is the optimal window for most deciduous trees. The tree is dormant, so it loses less energy from the cuts, and the approaching growth season means wounds begin to seal quickly.

There are important exceptions. Oak trees should only be pruned during the dormant season to reduce the risk of oak wilt transmission. Flowering cherries and dogwoods are best pruned immediately after they finish blooming in spring, since they set next year's flower buds on current-season growth. Fruit trees like apples and pears have their own specific timing depending on whether you are pruning for structure, fruit production, or disease management.

Our arborists schedule your pruning at the ideal time for your specific trees. We maintain a seasonal schedule and will remind you when it is time for your next service — one less thing to think about.

// FAQ

Trimming & pruning questions.

Common questions about tree trimming and pruning in Portland, OR. Still have questions? Get in touch.

Tree trimming generally refers to cutting back overgrown branches for aesthetic purposes and clearance — keeping branches away from your roof, power lines, or walkways. Pruning is more targeted and health-focused: removing dead, diseased, or crossing branches, thinning the crown for airflow, and making structural cuts that improve long-term form. Our ISA Certified Arborists handle both.

Most mature trees in Portland benefit from trimming every 3-5 years. Fast-growing species like bigleaf maples and cottonwoods may need attention every 2-3 years. Young trees should be structurally pruned every 1-2 years. Trees near structures or power lines may need more frequent maintenance. We offer free assessments to determine the right schedule for your trees.

Late winter through early spring (January-March) is ideal for most deciduous trees in Portland. Trees are dormant, wounds seal faster as growth resumes, and the bare canopy makes structure visible. However, dead or hazardous branches should be removed any time. Flowering trees like cherries are best pruned right after blooming in spring.

DIY trimming is dangerous and often causes more harm than good. Improper cuts like topping or flush cuts create entry points for disease and decay that can kill the tree. Working at height with saws is one of the most common causes of homeowner injury. Our arborists are trained in ANSI A300 pruning standards and use professional climbing and rigging techniques.
