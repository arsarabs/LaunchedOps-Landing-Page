The quiet leak

Three ways your current site is losing you jobs.

01

The first thirty seconds

A homeowner opens your site from Google, waits three seconds, sees a 2014 design, and closes the tab. The website is the interview. Most contractor sites fail it before the visitor reads a word.

02

The missed calls

Crews are working. Phones ring through to voicemail. Voicemail doesn’t get returned for hours. Every missed call is a homeowner who calls the next name on their list.

03

After five o’clock

Most homeowners search for contractors in the evening. If there’s no way to book, no way to get a price range, and no reply until Monday morning, the job is sold by sunrise.

Questions

What most contractors ask before booking a call.

What if I hate it?
You don’t pay. We take the staging site down and that’s the end of it. No “we worked 36 hours, you owe us 50%.” Zero dollars changes hands unless the site is live on your domain and you’re happy with it.
I already have a website. Do I need a new one?
Not always. If yours is newer, fast, and the content is doing work, keep it — we can add the automations and the review flow on top. Book a call and we’ll tell you honestly whether a rebuild is worth it.
Who owns the site?
You do. It’s on your domain, on hosting in your name, with the source files delivered to you any time you ask. No lock-in. If you fire us tomorrow, the site stays up and keeps working.
How do the automations actually work?
We wire your existing business number into a system that texts callers back when you miss, routes form fills to your phone in under a minute, sends calendar invites for booked appointments, and asks for Google reviews after each job. It runs in the background. You don’t log into anything.
Why only thirty-six hours?
Because building a contractor website isn’t a six-week project. We’ve built these before. The shape is known. The only variable is your content and your photos. We can build it in 36 hours, so you can get back to work while leads come in.
What’s the catch on “free until live”?
There isn’t one. We charge for the work once the site is up and you’ve approved it. Month-to-month after that. We earn our keep over time by being worth keeping around. If we’re not, you leave.
Where do you work?
Anywhere in the United States. Everything — the discovery call, the build, the revisions, the handoff — happens over phone, text, and screen share. No in-person meetings required.
